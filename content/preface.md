## Preface 
{:.no-label-increment}

This bachelor's thesis grew out of a long-standing interest in open-source software and digital sovereignty, and the conviction that the web does not have to be as centralised as it currently is. That interest took a more concrete direction during the course Web Topics Advanced, where a visit to imec-IDLab introduced me to the Solid ecosystem and the research being done there around personal data management. The connection between those technologies and the questions I had been asking about data ownership felt immediate, and when the opportunity arose to write my thesis at IDLab, I did not hesitate.

The work was conducted as a bachelor's thesis at the Odisee Hogeschool in Ghent, in collaboration with imec-IDLab in Ghent. Working within IDLab gave me access to a whole new world of ongoing research on personal data management, the Solid ecosystem, and Trustflows, all of which shaped this thesis from its earliest stages.

I want to thank Pieter Colpaert for welcoming me into his research group and for proposing the topic that became this thesis. Working with personal data management, the Solid ecosystem, and linked data quickly made clear how significant the research being done at IDLab is, and how much thought has gone into building a more human-centric web. During my internship Pieter consistently made time for feedback despite an exceptionally busy schedule, and he approached every idea I brought with genuine openness, never dismissing a suggestion simply because it came from a bachelor student. That attitude made a real difference to how I experienced the research process and made the work genuinely enjoyable. I also want to thank Bart Delrue for the day-to-day support and for the many conversations and follow-up meetings.

I owe particular thanks to Ben de Meester for helping me formulate the competency questions and for connecting me with the right people in the Flemish media industry. Through that connection a written questionnaire was completed by a practitioner at MediaNet Vlaanderen, whose perspective grounded the technical work in the realities of the sector and sharpened the design decisions documented here.

Finally, I thank my family and friends for their patience during the weeks when this thesis required most of my attention.

Michiel Van de Velde  
Ghent, May 2026
